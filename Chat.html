Question 1 : ~ i have ryzen3400g pc , 8gb ddr4 ram , 256ssd , 32gb usb drive can i convert this usb drive into ram to get 16 or 48gb ram ? Response 1 : ~ The short answer is technically yes, but practically it is a terrible idea that will make your computer much slower instead of faster. Here is why converting a USB drive into RAM (known as ReadyBoost or using it as a swap/page file) won't give you the performance boost you are looking for, along with better alternatives for your Ryzen 3400G setup. Why Using a USB Drive as RAM Doesn't Work Well 1. Massive Speed Difference: ◦ DDR4 RAM operates at speeds around 20,000 to 25,000 MB/s (Megabytes per second) with nanosecond response times. ◦ USB 3.0/3.1 flash drives max out at around 100 to 500 MB/s, and standard USB 2.0 drives are even slower (around 30 to 40 MB/s). ◦ More importantly, USB drives have high latency (delay), meaning the system will constantly freeze or stutter when trying to read/write data from the USB "RAM." 2. Hardware Wear and Tear: Flash memory (USB drives and SSDs) has a limited number of write cycles. Constantly using a USB drive as virtual memory will wear it out and cause it to fail very quickly. What You Can Do Instead Since you have a Ryzen 5 3400G (which is a great budget processor with built-in Radeon Vega graphics), here is how you can optimize your current setup: 1. Upgrade to 16GB of Physical RAM (Recommended) Your Ryzen 3400G has integrated graphics (Radeon RX Vega 11), which means your system automatically carves out a portion of your 8GB RAM (usually 2GB) to use as Video RAM (VRAM) for games and display. This leaves you with only about 6GB of usable system RAM, which fills up very fast. • Solution: Buying another 8GB stick of DDR4 RAM to match your existing one is usually very inexpensive and will give you a massive, night-and-day performance boost for multitasking and gaming. 2. Let Windows Manage Your SSD Page File Instead of a USB drive, your 256GB SSD is already being used by Windows as a virtual memory "page file." SSDs are vastly faster than USB drives. • Make sure your page file is enabled on your SSD so Windows can handle overflow memory safely without destroying your system's responsiveness.
